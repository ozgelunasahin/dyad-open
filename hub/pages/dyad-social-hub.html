The dyad look

Moss-black, never pure black (ground #131614, accent #7fbf9e). The wall: fine grain plus plaster mottle on every dark surface — the most recognizable thing we own. Serif voice (SangBleu Sunrise), mono for labels. Plain words, complete sentences, the zine register. Every visual should look like it was made by people who meet in rooms, not by a growth team. Paper, grain, handwriting, real photos with consent; never stock, never gradient-SaaS.

The strategy in one paragraph

Zero ad budget means the funnel cannot produce mass sign-ups, so the channel's job is not reach — it is making the founding counter move. The aim that survived the stress check: 175 founding members ≈ €4.2k/month; 125 by week 6 is on-track. The channels that convert are the personal ask and IRL events; YouTube and socials are the documentary layer that compounds into next quarter. Every piece of content ends on the counter, never on "link in bio."

Content pillars

PillarWhat it is
The experiment, in public"We're building a community its members govern. We don't know if it works. Watch." Consent rounds, the first ratification, the decision log — every real governance moment is an episode nobody can fake.
The counter"175 founding members keep dyad independent." Countdown updates, founding names (with consent), what the money sustains.
Rooms, not feedsConversation aftermath: two chairs, an empty café table, one anonymous feedback quote. The product is invisible; the meeting is the star.
The build logATProto experiment, open source, honest failures. Small audience — but the audience that funds, partners, and hires.

Tactics checklist

  • Founding page + public counter on the site (the campaign's engine)
  • Personal-ask script for founders (the highest-converting channel, ~25–35%)
  • Event capture kit: one photo per event, consent slip, one feedback quote
  • First 10 episodes mapped to Phase 0 governance moments (ratification = episode 1)
  • Weekly dashboard: sign-ups ~165/wk · founding members ~21/wk · ask-conversion (worry < 15%)
  • Berlin press list (Cee Cee, Mit Vergnügen, The Berliner) — pitch it, never plan on it
  • Referral mechanic visible after a good meeting, not at signup
  • Newsletter cadence tied to episodes, not to a calendar

What we do not do

  • No engagement bait; scarcity stated plainly, once (175 seats are real)
  • No growth loops that reward screen time — success is meetings held and feedback quality
  • No content showing members without explicit consent
  • No pressure cadence: long-tenure subscribers convert at 3–5× — patience over pressure
