## 1 · The dyad look

What we already have, so every asset starts from the same place:

- **Moss-black, not black.** Ground `#131614`, surface `#1b201d`, ink `#e7eae5`, accent `#7fbf9e` / `#9ad0b4`. All blacks are green-tinted.
- **The wall.** Fine grain + large plaster mottle over every dark surface (implemented across app, landing, zine). Assets for socials should carry the same texture — it is the most recognizable thing we own.
- **Serif voice.** SangBleu Sunrise for the words; mono for labels and small structure. Headings sentence case ("Collectively owned / offline social network").
- **Plain words.** Complete sentences, no aphorisms, no em-dashes (the zine register). The voice *is* the brand.

**Rule of thumb:** every visual should look like it was made by people who meet in rooms, not by a growth team. Paper, grain, handwriting, real photos with consent — never stock, never gradient-SaaS.

## 3 · The strategy in one paragraph

Zero ad budget means the funnel cannot produce mass sign-ups, so the channel's job is **not reach — it is making the founding counter move**. The aim that survived the stress check: **175 founding members ≈ €4.2k/month**; 125 by week 6 is on-track. The two channels that actually convert are the personal ask and IRL events; YouTube + socials are the *documentary layer* that compounds into next quarter. Every piece of content ends on the counter, not on "link in bio."

## 4 · Content pillars (the documentary layer)

1. **The experiment, in public.** "We're building a community its members govern. We don't know if it works. Watch." Consent rounds, the first ratification, the decision log — each real governance moment is an episode. Nobody can fake this content; it only exists because the practice is real.
2. **The counter.** "175 founding members keep dyad independent." Countdown updates, founding-member names (with consent), what the money sustains (the open wage line, when it comes).
3. **Rooms, not feeds.** Conversation aftermath: two chairs, an empty café table, one pull-quote from feedback (anonymous, with consent). The product is invisible; the meeting is the star.
4. **The build log.** ATProto experiment, open source, honest failures. Small audience, but it is the audience that funds (Skyseed), partners (Blacksky), and hires.

## 5 · Tactics checklist

- [ ] Founding page + public counter on the site (the campaign's engine)
- [ ] Personal-ask script for founders (the highest-converting channel: ~25–35%)
- [ ] Event capture kit: one photo per event, consent slip, one feedback quote
- [ ] First 10 episodes mapped to Phase 0 governance moments (ratification = episode 1)
- [ ] Weekly dashboard: sign-ups (~165/wk aim) · founding members (~21/wk aim) · ask-conversion (worry < 15%)
- [ ] Berlin press list (Cee Cee, Mit Vergnügen, The Berliner) — pitch, never plan on it
- [ ] Referral mechanic visible at the right moment (after a good meeting, not at signup)
- [ ] Substack/newsletter cadence tied to episodes, not to a calendar

## 6 · What we do not do

- No engagement bait, no countdown *pressure* (scarcity is real: 175 seats, stated plainly, once).
- No growth loops that reward screen time — pilot success is measured in meetings held and feedback quality.
- No content that shows members without explicit consent.
- The long-tenure finding from the research: free subscribers convert at 3–5× after they've stayed a while. So: patience over pressure, in copy and in cadence.
